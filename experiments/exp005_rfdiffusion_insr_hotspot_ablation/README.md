# exp005: RFdiffusion insulin-receptor hotspot ablation

## Status

**Completed 2026-08-15.** The comparison reuses the ten completed
hotspot-conditioned designs from `exp003` and `exp004` and contains ten matched
designs generated without hotspot labels.

## Question

When target structure, contig, complex checkpoint, seeds and denoiser settings
are held fixed, how does removing the three target hotspot labels change the
location and geometry of RFdiffusion-generated binder backbones?

This is a conditioning ablation, not a test of binding affinity. In particular,
the hotspot-free complex model does not calculate binding free energy or
iteratively discover the experimentally best binding site.

## Matched design

Both arms use:

- the same insulin-receptor example target, residues A1–A150;
- contig `[A1-150/0 70-100]`;
- RFdiffusion complex checkpoint and 50 reverse steps;
- deterministic design indices/seeds 42–51;
- zero additional Cα and frame noise;
- the same software, checkpoint and schedule cache.

The sole intended scientific difference is:

| Arm | Hotspot input | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| guided | `[A59,A83,A91]` | completed outputs from `exp003` and `exp004` |
| unguided | omitted (`None`) | new outputs generated by this experiment |

RFdiffusion's own implementation warns that the complex model was trained with
hotspot features and is being used without specifying them. The unguided arm is
therefore best interpreted as a useful ablation of the control signal, not a
validated pocket-discovery mode.

## Prespecified comparisons

Every final structure was aligned on all 150 target Cα atoms. We then
compare:

1. the minimum binder-Cα distance to each nominal hotspot;
2. the distance between the binder center and hotspot centroid;
3. the contacted target-residue set at a 10 Å Cα threshold;
4. the closest contacted target residue and interchain backbone clashes;
5. target-relative pose and intrinsic binder shape for matched seeds.

The notebook shows paired guided/unguided structures and overlaid ensembles.
The analysis does not select the visually most compelling example after the
fact: all ten paired seeds are retained.

## Result

The hotspot-guided design had the smaller minimum binder-Cα distance to one of
A59, A83 or A91 in 9/10 matched seeds. The median was 6.38 Å with hotspot
conditioning and 9.72 Å without it. Matched generated poses often changed
substantially (median target-aligned symmetric Chamfer distance 9.46 Å), while
neither arm contained interchain backbone atom pairs below 2 Å.

The binder-center-to-hotspot-centroid distance was not consistently smaller in
the guided arm. The supported conclusion is therefore local: the hotspot
feature steers at least part of the generated interface toward nominated
target residues. It does not pin the whole binder around the hotspot centroid.

See the reviewed result in
`reports/exp005_rfdiffusion_insr_hotspot_ablation/README.md` and the rotatable
paired/ensemble views in `notebooks/real_rfdiffusion_proteinmpnn_insr.ipynb`.

## Interpretation boundary

A hotspot-free structure contacting a different target region does not show
that RFdiffusion found a better pocket. Conversely, a hotspot-guided structure
near A59/A83/A91 only shows that the conditioning affected geometry; it does not
show favorable affinity, specificity or experimental binding.
