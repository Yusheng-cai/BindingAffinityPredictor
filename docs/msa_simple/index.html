What is an MSA?

Simple version first MSA (Multiple Sequence Alignment) means lining up similar protein sequences so equivalent positions can be compared directly.

Think of it like this: take three similar sentences and insert spaces so shared words stay in the same column.

Unaligned (hard to compare)

Rosa likes apples
Rosa really likes red apples
Rosa likes pie

Aligned (easy to compare)

Rosa likes  apples
Rosa really likes red apples
Rosa likes -- pie--

This lets us say where a letter/word is inserted, missing, or changed across related proteins.

Toy protein MSA example

Toy amino-acid letters (not a real protein).

Before alignment

>protA
MAFELKQ

>protB
MAFKQ

>protC
MAFELQ

After alignment

M A F E L K Q
M A F - - K Q
M A F E L - Q

Gaps (-) are inserted so residues are put into comparable columns.

Conserved positions are highlighted:

M A F E L K Q
M A F - - K Q
M A F E L - Q

Why Boltz-2 cares about MSA

Practical meaning for your project.

1For one protein family, MSA tells Boltz-2 which positions are evolutionarily conserved.
2Conserved positions often correspond to structural/functional constraints (fold and active-site residues).
3Boltz-2 can use this signal to improve structure likelihood and potentially ligand placement quality.
A warning: MSA is only evolutionary signal from sequence databases. It does not encode ligand atoms or binding constants by itself.

Small practical check for our benchmark

For your TYK2 smoke test, if you run with --use_msa_server, Boltz-2 fetches a protein MSA from ColabFold/MMseqs2 online and saves it locally before sampling structures. Without it, the model can still run (with less context).
