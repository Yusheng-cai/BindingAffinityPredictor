Project briefing · August 2026

From backbone generation to physics-guided binder design

Understanding RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN, deciding where solvent physics belongs, and choosing a first experiment that can genuinely fail.

Read: choose “Read” above  ·  Present: arrows navigate, S opens notes
The goal is not simply to run RFdiffusion. The project asks whether a solvent-aware physical signal helps choose better binders from a controlled candidate set. Emphasize that every layer has a different output and a different evidential status.

The thesis in one sentence

Hold generation fixed; test whether physics improves selection.

Falsifiable question

Given the same target epitope, the same RFdiffusion backbones, and the same ProteinMPNN sequence pools, does a hydration-aware interface score enrich binders with lower experimental Kd?

Not the first claim“We can predict absolute binding free energy.”
Not sufficient evidenceOne attractive structure or one high confidence score.
What would countA prospective, matched comparison against strong and simple baselines.
The physics model is tested as an incremental decision rule, not protected by changes elsewhere in the pipeline.
This matched design is the conceptual center of the proposal. If the backbone pool or sequence pool differs by method, an apparent gain cannot be attributed to the new score.

Terminology: in the rest of this talk, “binder” means a newly designed protein chain, while “target” is the protein it should bind.

Watson et al., Nature (2023) · Dauparas et al., Nature Methods (2025) · Vázquez Torres et al., Nature Methods (2025)
This distinction prevents a common conceptual error. ProteinMPNN does not model ligand atoms. LigandMPNN is the relevant inverse-folding model when the context contains a small molecule, metal or nucleic acid.

02 · RFdiffusion

A conditional generator of protein backbone geometry

Input

  • Target protein coordinates
  • Requested binder length or contig
  • Optional target “hotspot” residues
  • Optional fixed motifs / topology constraints
  • Random seed and sampling settings

iterative equivariant denoising

t = T⟶t = 0

Output

  • Binder backbone coordinates
  • Target-relative binding pose
  • A reverse denoising trajectory
  • Internal confidence values

Not a designed sequence.
Not a Kd.

Learned taskpredict a clean residue-frame arrangement from a noisy one, conditioned on the target
RFdiffusion paper · official implementation
A residue frame contains translation and orientation information derived from backbone atoms. The target remains conditioning context; the binder is the part being generated in the usual complex-design setup.

Why equivariance?

The answer should rotate when the molecular system rotates

rotate by R
⟶
f(Rx + a) = R f(x) + aA global rotation R and translation a must change coordinates consistently—not change the protein design problem.

Equivariance is a geometric constraint on the network. It does not mean that a protein is physically symmetric.

This distinction often resolves the confusion. Invariance would mean the output does not change under rotation, suitable for a scalar score. Equivariance means a coordinate output transforms with the input.

03 · ProteinMPNN

Inverse folding: sequence conditioned on backbone

  1. GraphResidues are nodes; spatial neighbors form edges.
  2. GeometryBackbone atoms define relative distances and orientations.
  3. MessagesEncoder layers aggregate local structural context.
  4. DecodeThe model emits probabilities over 20 amino acids.
  5. SampleDifferent orders, seeds and temperatures yield sequence diversity.
Native objectiveP(S | X,T) = ∏i P(si | X,T,Sprevious)likelihood ≠ affinity
Dauparas et al., Science (2022) · official implementation
ProteinMPNN learns which sequences are compatible with protein backbone geometry. It was trained using structure-derived sequence recovery, not measured Kd values. Its log probability is therefore a sequence prior, not a binding assay prediction.

04 · Where physics can enter

Start with reranking; integrate only after the score survives

Level 1 · first

Generate → pack → score → rerank

The physical model sees complete candidate interfaces but does not alter RFdiffusion or ProteinMPNN.

  • works with nondifferentiable simulations
  • clean matched comparison
  • easy failure diagnosis
Level 2

Bias sequence decoding

Modify amino-acid logits using a local chemical or hydration term.

ℓguidedi(a) = ℓMPNNi(a) − βΔEi(a)
  • more sampling-efficient
  • requires local credit assignment
Level 3

Guide backbone diffusion

Add gradients or auxiliary potentials during denoising so backbone geometry responds to physics.

  • can repair geometric bottlenecks
  • hardest to stabilize and validate
  • generator may exploit score defects
Pguided(S|X,T) ∝ PMPNN(S|X,T) exp[−βEphys(S,X,T)]Useful mathematical target; not automatically a calibrated thermodynamic ensemble.
This sequencing is methodological, not timid. A new score should first demonstrate predictive value on completed candidates. Guidance can magnify even a subtle score pathology through reward hacking.

The Patel-group bridge

Hydrophobicity is an emergent water response—not a carbon count

Specialized simulations displace waters from chosen protein-surface regions and measure how readily a cavity forms.

Published observationAcross five studied proteins, roughly 60–70% of interface contacts—but only about 10–20% of noncontacts—nucleated cavities under the chosen perturbation.

Polar and charged atoms can belong to a hydrophobic patch because nanoscale chemistry and topography act collectively.

Project opportunity: ask whether displacing weakly held interfacial water adds information beyond buried area, contact counts, and learned structure confidence.
Rego, Xi & Patel, PNAS (2021)
Be precise: the PNAS work supports interface identification, not an affinity predictor. Our proposed extension is a hypothesis. The reported percentages describe the five systems and the paper's perturbation protocol, not a universal classifier performance.

Do not oversell a hand-built equation. This slide defines feature families. A useful first version may be an interpretable score with only a few terms plus an ensemble estimate across packed conformations.

Thermodynamic boundary

An interface score is not automatically ΔGbind

ΔG°bind = −RT ln(KaC°) = RT ln(Kd/C°)
EnsemblesBound and unbound conformations, not one minimized pose
SolventWater rearrangement, ions, dielectric response, and protonation
EntropyTranslational, rotational, backbone, and side-chain freedom
Standard stateA defined concentration convention is part of the thermodynamics
Until calibrated against a defined endpoint, report an interface-quality score or binding proxy—not kcal mol−1, Kd, or “predicted affinity.”
The difference between a ranking proxy and a free energy is central for a molecular-simulation audience. Even an explicit-solvent hydration term captures only one contribution unless embedded in a rigorous thermodynamic cycle.

Use hydration maps, MaSIF-like surface fingerprints, conservation, or pocket/site predictors to nominate regions.

Requires site-level validation
No prior site

Multi-site exploration

Generate against several surface patches—or use a method such as BindCraft that can search without specified hotspots.

Most confounded first experiment

A failure at the end does not reveal which upstream hypothesis failed. For the first score-validation study, choose a known, functionally testable site.

hydration patches · MaSIF · BindCraft
This answers the templated-atoms question. Original RFdiffusion hotspots are target residue labels, not ligand atoms. If no site is known, one can sample broadly, but that changes the scientific question from score validation to simultaneous site discovery and design.

This is a decision matrix, not a universal target ranking. TmAFP is scientifically rich but its interface is ice, not a soluble protein partner, so the experimental endpoint and design architecture differ from a standard binder campaign.

Recommended system

TEM-1 gives affinity, function, and specificity in one compact system

Structural anchor

TEM-1 has a known active-site-blocking protein partner, BLIP, and a well-characterized complex structure.

Dynamic range

BLIP binds TEM-1 near 1.3 nM but much more weakly to homologous SHV-1, giving a specificity countertarget.

Functional readout

If the designed binder occludes the active site, nitrocefin turnover provides an orthogonal inhibition assay.

Important caveat

The natural BLIP interface and related structures may be represented in training data; this pilot tests our ranking increment, not de novo generalization of RFdiffusion.

Albeck & Schreiber, binding biophysics (1999) · Reynolds et al., interface redesign (2008) · Hanes et al., specificity (2011)
We are not trying to beat natural BLIP initially. We generate de novo binders to a known functional surface and compare ranking methods within that pool. SHV-1 provides a close structural countertarget for specificity.

What can go wrong?

Failure modes are scientifically useful if they remain separable

No soluble protein

Sequence prior or developability failure—not evidence about binding physics.

Inspect expression, aggregation, and monomer prediction.
Folds but does not bind

Interface geometry, side-chain packing, or site hypothesis may be wrong.

Use complex prediction, site mutants, and alternative backbones.
Binds but does not inhibit

May bind outside the active-site mechanism or with unproductive geometry.

Competition and epitope mapping become decisive.
Score “wins” in silico only

Reward hacking or a baseline-confounded metric.

Prospective blinding and simple baselines expose this.
Strong but nonspecific binding

Sticky surface rather than complementary recognition.

SHV-1 and unrelated proteins test negative design.
Initial state changes score

Rotamer, water, protonation, or conformational sampling is incomplete.

Replicated ensembles and convergence diagnostics are mandatory.
The final item connects directly to the user's molecular-simulation concern: a score based on one packing or one solvent configuration is not robust. Replicates and ensemble summaries should be part of the method, not post hoc troubleshooting.

Roadmap

Four gates from understanding to evidence

Gate 0 · done

Understand the representations

Run RFdiffusion, inspect trajectories, generate ProteinMPNN sequences, and visualize structural diversity.

Gate 1

Freeze the scoring hypothesis

Define hydration and control terms, ensemble protocol, output units, and failure rules before looking at prospective results.

Gate 2

Retrospective calibration

Use known interfaces such as MDM2 and BLIP complexes to check sign, robustness, and nontrivial value beyond surface area.

Gate 3

TEM-1 prospective pilot

Generate one common pool, select blinded matched arms, then measure folding, Kd, inhibition, and specificity.

Gate 4

Generalize—or stop

Freeze the method before a second target. A second system is needed before claiming general utility.

A go/no-go decision belongs at every gate. Retrospective fit alone is not evidence. If the hydration score adds nothing beyond transparent baselines, that is a valuable and publishable constraint on the hypothesis.

Take-home message

The project is a controlled test of added physical information

1

RFdiffusion generates target-conditioned binder backbones; it does not predict affinity.

2

ProteinMPNN samples sequences compatible with a backbone; its likelihood is not Kd.

3

Our first contribution should be a hydration-aware, ensemble-robust reranker tested on a common candidate pool.

Starter system TEM-1 active-site binder design

with SHV-1 specificity, direct binding, and enzyme inhibition as separable experimental endpoints

A successful pilot would justify guided sequence design. It would not yet establish an absolute free-energy predictor.

End on the experimental logic. Invite discussion on the scoring observable, synthesis budget, and assay format—not on whether a pretty generated structure “looks convincing.”
