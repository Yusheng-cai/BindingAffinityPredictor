Alchemical free energy · visual field guide

When can we trust an FEP calculation?

A free energy is a state function. A finite simulation remembers where it started. The gap between those statements is the central convergence problem in protein–ligand FEP.

The question to ask
Did the calculation sample the equilibrium ensemble—or only the metastable basin supplied by its initial protein, ligand pose, and water network? LITERATURE SNAPSHOT · 2026-08-07

Core conclusion: FEP is formally rigorous only when each alchemical state is sampled from equilibrium. Good λ overlap, many replica round trips, and a small MBAR error do not prove that slow coordinates orthogonal to λ have equilibrated.

01 · The actual problem

One λ coordinate, many hidden coordinates

The integration variable λ changes the Hamiltonian. It is not automatically a useful reaction coordinate for a pocket-opening motion, a ligand torsion, or a buried-water exchange.

Zλ = ∫ d𝐱 exp[−βUλ(𝐱)] = Σs Zλ,s
Gλ = −kBT ln(Σs Zλ,s)

Here 𝐱 contains the protein, ligand, solvent and ions. The index s labels metastable substates: wet/dry pockets, alternative poses, side-chain rotamers, loop conformations, and so on.

In the infinite-sampling limitEvery metastable basin contributes with its Boltzmann weight. The result is independent of the starting coordinates.
In a finite trajectoryIf a run never leaves basin s, it estimates the restricted quantity −kT ln Zλ,s, not the complete free energy.
The practical consequenceAn A-initialized and B-initialized calculation can follow different restricted ensembles and disagree by several kcal/mol.

02 · The FEP procedure

Alchemy avoids binding—but not equilibrium

04 · Buried water example

The water has to leave—or arrive

A local hydration state can be effectively frozen on the simulation timescale, even though the complete simulation box contains explicit water.

Ligand A · smaller

H₂O

A stable or metastable buried water occupies the space beside A.

⇄slow exchange through a narrow or gated pathway

Ligand B · larger

B displaces the water. In reverse, the newly opened cavity must become correctly hydrated.

A → B failure

The ligand grows, but the trapped water cannot escape. The complex leg accumulates an artificial steric or hydration penalty.

B → A failure

The ligand shrinks, but the buried cavity remains dry because ordinary MD cannot insert water across the kinetic barrier.

Specialized remedies

Grand-canonical Monte Carlo inserts/deletes waters; NCMC/BLUES proposes large water moves; occupancy biases can be used when the relevant site is known.

05 · What methods can—and cannot—fix

No universal enhanced sampler

Different methods target different barriers. The method must actually couple to the slow coordinate that controls equilibration.

HREX along λ

Improves communication among alchemical states and often helps local relaxation. It cannot guarantee motion along a coordinate weakly coupled to λ.

FEP/REST or REST2

Scales a selected “hot” ligand/pocket region to accelerate local reorganization. Distant protein motions and water-number changes may remain slow.

Explicit CV biasing

Umbrella sampling, metadynamics or ABF can accelerate a known torsion, pocket opening or occupancy coordinate—but may miss another orthogonal barrier.

GCMC / NCMC water moves

Directly targets difficult water occupancy and rearrangement. It still requires a properly defined region, chemical potential and sufficient sampling.

Multiple-state enumeration

Run distinct poses, protonation states or conformers and combine their partition functions. This is rigorous if the relevant states have actually been enumerated.

Longer ordinary MD

Useful when the slow timescale is reachable. Simply extending one trapped trajectory does not provide controlled uncertainty or prove a transition will occur.

06 · Evidence hierarchy

What we should inspect before trusting ΔΔG

No single diagnostic is sufficient. The most convincing case combines alchemical, structural, hydration, replication and network evidence.

Independent initial ensemblesA-bound and B-bound structures; distinct poses, water networks, solvations and velocities where relevant.
Between-replica varianceUse independent repeats; do not rely only on an internal MBAR uncertainty from one trajectory ensemble.
MBAR overlap connectivityEvery endpoint must be connected through overlapping adjacent λ distributions. This checks λ—not all of configuration space.
Replica mixing and round tripsConfigurations should traverse alchemical space repeatedly without isolated λ cliques.
Physical-coordinate distributionsCompare ligand torsions and poses, pocket rotamers, loop coordinates and other mechanistically relevant observables.
Water occupancy and networksCompare P(Nwater), residence, bridging-water geometry and wet/dry transitions across initializations.
Time-block stabilityRepeat the estimate on trajectory fractions, while remembering that a permanently trapped trajectory can look stable.
Thermodynamic-cycle closureFor A→B→C→A, the signed sum should be zero. A large residual detects inconsistency; a small one can arise from cancellation.
Alternative paths or mappingsWhere feasible, test whether different valid alchemical constructions agree within uncertainty.
Prospective experimentInternal convergence tests sampling under one force field; experimental comparison also tests model and setup error.

Asymmetric logic: large endpoint dependence or cycle error is evidence of failure. Small endpoint dependence or cycle error is not proof of correctness—both calculations may share the same unsampled basin, force-field error, protonation mistake or topology artifact.

07 · Proposed project standard

A practical reversibility experiment

For a challenging ligand pair, report every result with the common sign convention GB−GA.

ENSEMBLE A

A-like initialization

Begin from an A-bound structure and an A-appropriate hydration/pose state. Prepare at least three genuinely independent replicas.

ENSEMBLE B

B-like initialization

Begin from a B-bound structure and a B-appropriate hydration/pose state. Match force field and thermodynamic settings.

ALCHEMICAL CHECKS

λ overlap and exchange

Inspect MBAR overlap, exchange probabilities, replica trajectories, round trips and time-dependent estimates for every leg and repeat.

PHYSICAL CHECKS

Compare ensemble distributions

Measure pose, torsion, protein and hydration distributions—not only final ΔG values. Quantify distribution overlap where useful.

REDUNDANCY

Build cycles

Add a third ligand or alternative transformation so thermodynamic closure supplies an independent internal-consistency constraint.

ESCALATION

Target the observed barrier

If ensembles disagree, identify the coordinate retaining memory and choose a matching remedy: REST, metadynamics, GCMC/NCMC, enumeration, or explicit conformational free energy.
