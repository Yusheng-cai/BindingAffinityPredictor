Independent evaluation · source-critical summary

Out of ten RFdiffusion designs, how many might actually work?

RFdiffusion proposes a binder backbone. ProteinMPNN proposes sequences for that backbone. Neither model directly predicts an experimental probability of binding. The only defensible estimate comes from following the full computational-and-experimental funnel.

≠

Backbone confidence is not binding probability

The approximately 0.99 RFdiffusion confidence values in our ten structures cannot rank experimental binding. ProteinMPNN likelihood scores measure sequence compatibility with a supplied backbone; they are not affinities, free energies, or probabilities of laboratory success.

02 · Define the endpoint

“Success” changes at every stage

A percentage is uninterpretable unless its denominator and endpoint are stated.

  1. 1

    Generated backbone

    RFdiffusion produced coordinates with the requested target, hotspot, and length conditioning. This is an engineering completion criterion.

    Our state: 10
  2. 2

    Sequence-designable backbone

    At least one ProteinMPNN sequence independently refolds close to the intended backbone.

    Not measured
  3. 3

    Predicted complex

    An independent structure predictor supports the intended target-bound pose and interface with prespecified confidence thresholds.

    Not measured
  4. 4

    Experimental binder

    The expressed protein produces target-dependent binding signal under a defined assay concentration and threshold.

    Not measured
  5. 5

    Useful binder

    Affinity, specificity, folding, expression, oligomeric state, stability, and biological function satisfy the intended application.

    Not measured

04 · Paper-by-paper summaries

What each study contributes—and what it cannot establish

The studies answer different questions. ProteinMPNN evaluates sequence design; RFdiffusion evaluates backbone generation; the independent studies examine whether filtered candidates reproduce in new laboratories.

Backbone generation→Sequence design→Structure filtering→Experimental binding
Peer reviewedMethods paper
01

Dauparas et al. · Science · 2022 [2]

ProteinMPNN: which sequence fits a supplied backbone?

Question
Can a graph neural network assign amino-acid sequences that encode a given protein backbone more robustly and quickly than Rosetta?
Method
Residues are nodes in a spatial graph. The model samples residue identities using the backbone geometry and neighboring sequence context.
Main result
Native-sequence recovery was 52.4%, versus 32.9% for Rosetta. Experimental structures and functional tests showed broad utility, including rescue of previously failed designs.
Boundary
ProteinMPNN likelihood is sequence–backbone compatibility—not binding affinity, free energy, or probability of experimental success.

For our project: use several sequences per backbone; do not rank binders by ProteinMPNN score alone.

Peer reviewedDeveloper study
02

Bennett et al. · Nature Communications · 2023 [3]

AlphaFold filtering: reject candidates that do not refold or dock as designed

Question
Can structure-prediction networks identify two common failures: the sequence does not adopt the designed fold, or the folded binder does not form the intended interface?
Method
Approximately one million previously tested designs were analyzed retrospectively, followed by prospective campaigns. Binder RMSD, pLDDT and interchain pAE were evaluated as filters.
Main result
AlphaFold/RoseTTAFold filtering enriched experimental success by nearly tenfold. Interface pAE below about 10 was especially useful. ProteinMPNN was much faster than Rosetta sequence design without reducing observed hit rate.
Boundary
Absolute success remained below 1% in these precursor campaigns, and one target site produced no binder. Prediction is enrichment, not proof.

For our project: independent refolding and complex prediction is the necessary next filter after ProteinMPNN.

Peer reviewedOriginal RFdiffusion
03

Watson et al. · Nature · 2023 [1]

RFdiffusion: generate a backbone around a chosen target surface

Question
Can a RoseTTAFold-derived denoising model generate new, designable protein structures and target-conditioned protein binders?
Method
RFdiffusion denoises residue positions and orientations; ProteinMPNN supplies sequences; AlphaFold2 filters designs; 95 selected candidates per protein target were tested by BLI.
Main result
Across five binder targets, the filtered set had a 19% experimental hit rate. All five yielded binders. An HA complex matched the design with 0.63 Å backbone RMSD. The matching zero-noise InsR calculation reported 17.9% in-silico success.
Boundary
The 19% refers to selected, computationally filtered candidates—not raw backbones. The 10 µM single-point screen could count weak interactions, and this was the model developers' study.

For our project: this is the closest same-target reference, but its experimental percentage is an optimistic comparison for our current unfiltered ten.

Technical reportIndependent re-test
04

Zambaldi et al. · arXiv technical report · 2024 [4]

AlphaProteo study: resynthesize published RFdiffusion designs

Question
How does AlphaProteo compare experimentally with prior methods, and do published RFdiffusion candidates reproduce under one standardized external assay?
Method
Google DeepMind synthesized 95 published RFdiffusion sequences for each of IL-7Rα, PD-L1 and TrkA and tested them by yeast display at 0.5–1 µM target.
Main result
RFdiffusion hit rates were 16.8%, 12.6% and 0%, respectively. AlphaProteo itself succeeded on seven targets but failed on a deliberately difficult eighth target, TNFα.
Boundary
Assay format and concentration differed from the original 10 µM BLI screen. This is the strongest independent re-test here, but remains a non-peer-reviewed technical report.

For our project: even 95 filtered designs can yield zero binders; target identity and assay definition dominate any universal percentage.

PreprintIndependent small study
05

Jiang et al. · bioRxiv · 2025 [7]

Biochemical detection: what happens in a small five-design campaign?

Question
Can small RFdiffusion design sets provide practical affinity reagents for Western blotting and biochemical detection?
Method
Five designs were evaluated for Strep-tag II and for each of five eukaryotic protein targets: STAT3, FGF4, EGF, PDGF-BB and CD4.
Main result
Two Strep-tag designs showed activity but did not match conventional antibodies. None of the 25 designs against the five protein targets was satisfactory; failure modes included expression, specificity and undetectable affinity.
Boundary
Five candidates per target provide weak statistical power. The application and implementation differ from the original benchmark, and the report is not peer reviewed.

For our project: it is direct evidence that a small set of attractive structures can reasonably produce zero useful reagents.

PreprintCrowdsourced benchmark
06

Cotet et al. · bioRxiv · 2025 [8]

EGFR competition: methods matter, and the leaderboard is not a controlled benchmark

Question
What can be learned when many groups use diverse computational strategies to design EGFR binders under a common experimental measurement pipeline?
Method
More than 1,800 designs were submitted over two rounds; 601 selected proteins were experimentally characterized. Workflows included RFdiffusion–ProteinMPNN, hallucination, known-binder redesign and antibody optimization.
Main result
Sixty binders were validated. The selected-design hit rate improved from roughly 3% to 13–14% between rounds, while expression rose from 73% to 95%. Most second-round de novo hits came from hallucination workflows; one came from RFdiffusion.
Boundary
Selection was non-random, workflows were heterogeneous and many entries began from known binders. The overall hit rate cannot be assigned to RFdiffusion.

For our project: expert candidate selection and protocol details can matter as much as the named generative model.

Peer reviewedDeveloper follow-up
07

Sappington et al. · Nature Communications · 2026 [6]

β-pairing conditioning: target chemistry changes designability

Question
Can explicit β-strand pairing geometry improve binder design against polar, exposed target edge strands that standard hotspot conditioning handles poorly?
Method
RFdiffusion was conditioned on binder–target β-strand pairing. In a comparison, 500 backbones per method and ten ProteinMPNN sequences per backbone were filtered computationally.
Main result
Standard hotspot conditioning had a 0.98% in-silico pass rate versus 9.2% with β-pairing conditioning. Experimentally validated binders spanned picomolar to mid-nanomolar affinity, and a KIT complex closely matched its design.
Boundary
Experimental effort ranged from 96 candidates to libraries of thousands, and some difficult targets had below-1% strong-hit rates. The study comes from the broader RFdiffusion development ecosystem.

For our project: “RFdiffusion success rate” is not a model constant; epitope geometry, polarity and conditioning can alter it by an order of magnitude.

Synthesis across all seven

The robust conclusion is a funnel, not a single accuracy number.

  1. Generate: RFdiffusion samples plausible target-conditioned backbones.
  2. Sequence: ProteinMPNN assigns sequences compatible with each geometry.
  3. Filter: an independent structure predictor rejects many fold and interface failures.
  4. Measure: expression, aggregation, specificity and concentration-dependent binding remain experimental questions.

Practical interpretation

Expect 1–2 computational survivors and plan experimentally for 0–1 binders.

If the naïvely multiplied 3.58% per-backbone rate were a true independent probability, ten trials would have a 30.6% chance of at least one hit and a 69.4% chance of none. Those probabilities are shown only to make the arithmetic tangible.

Why the arithmetic is fragile

  • The computational and experimental rates come from selected stages, not a single randomized prospective experiment.
  • Backbones share one target and epitope, so failures can be correlated.
  • ProteinMPNN samples on one backbone are correlated, not independent trials.
  • Assay format, target concentration, expression system, aggregation, and threshold alter the measured hit rate.
  • The original study targeted relatively non-polar sites and warned that polar sites can be harder.

06 · The decisive next experiment

Run a prespecified computational funnel on all ten

The purpose is enrichment, not proof. Wet-lab measurements remain the ground truth for binding.

1

Sequence

Generate eight ProteinMPNN sequences per backbone while fixing the receptor: 80 sequence–backbone candidates.

2

Refold independently

Predict each designed monomer and target complex with a sequence-based model that did not generate the backbone.

3

Apply frozen criteria

Reproduce the classic AF2 filter: binder pLDDT >80, interface pAE <10, and binder RMSD to design <1 Å.

4

Check the interface

Inspect clashes, buried surface, shape complementarity, unsatisfied polar atoms, exposed hydrophobics, and agreement across predictors.

5

Preserve diversity

Select representatives from distinct backbone and interface clusters rather than eight near-duplicate sequences from one fold.

6

Measure binding

Test expression and monodispersity, then perform concentration-dependent BLI or SPR with negative, competition, and specificity controls.

Why independent structure prediction is useful

The sequence-design problem asks whether a sampled amino-acid sequence actually encodes the RFdiffusion backbone and its intended complex. AlphaFold filtering was shown to enrich experimental binders in the pre-RFdiffusion pipeline, but it is an imperfect classifier and cannot substitute for affinity measurement. Bennett et al., 2023
