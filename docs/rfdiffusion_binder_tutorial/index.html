“Contact” means a target residue has any binder Cα within 10 Å. “Confidence” is RFdiffusion's internal pLDDT-like output—not affinity.

Intrinsic shape

What changes if pose is removed?

Unequal-length Cα traces are resampled to 64 equally spaced arc-length points and independently Kabsch-aligned. Their RMSD describes curve-shape difference. It is deliberately not called TM-score.

Target-relative pose

What changes around the target?

After aligning all 150 target Cα atoms, symmetric nearest-neighbor distance compares binder point clouds. This retains changes in binder location, orientation and shape while tolerating unequal lengths.

Interface footprint

Are the same target residues contacted?

Jaccard similarity compares target-residue contact sets. A value of 1 means identical sets; 0 means no overlap. The observed median is 0.50, despite the same three hotspot labels.

Stage 1 · Input specification

The target structure stays fixed

RFdiffusion receives the coordinates of insulin-receptor residues A1–A150. Residues A59, A83 and A91 indicate the desired surface region.

What the model has

Target residue identities, backbone coordinates and hotspot labels.

What it does not have

A binder backbone, binder sequence, affinity or experimental measurement.

Key idea: A hotspot is spatial guidance—not a force field and not a known energetic hot spot.

05 · A common conceptual trap

Training and generation run in opposite directions

During training

Known structures are deliberately corrupted

native complex→ add noise at tnoisy frames→ learn denoisingclean estimate

The model sees examples derived from experimentally determined protein structures. Residue coordinate frames are perturbed at different noise levels, and the network learns how protein-like geometry can be recovered while using structural context.

During our run

A new binder begins from noise

random frames→ repeated denoisingcoherent chain→ outputnew backbone

The target coordinates remain conditioned and fixed. Only the requested binder chain is generated. The network repeatedly estimates a cleaner structure and advances from noise level t toward zero.

More precise than “moving atoms downhill”: RFdiffusion operates on residue frames containing translations and rotations, using an SE(3)-equivariant structure network. The animation does not represent molecular dynamics, an energy minimization trajectory, or physical time.

07 · What each model computes

Backbone generation is not sequence design

∿

RFdiffusion

Structure generator

  • Conditions on: target coordinates, contigs and hotspot labels.
  • Generates: backbone positions and orientations for a new chain.
  • Output convention: generated residues are written as glycine placeholders.
  • Does not report: a physical binding free energy.
backbone coordinates→
ACDE

ProteinMPNN

Inverse-folding sequence model

  • Conditions on: the fixed backbone and neighboring residues in three dimensions.
  • Represents: residues as a geometric graph with spatial neighbor edges.
  • Generates: amino-acid probabilities and sampled sequences.
  • Decoding: follows sampled orders, not necessarily N-terminus to C-terminus.

08 · Validation and evidence

What can we conclude from a predicted complex?

1

Generation completed

The code ran and produced a PDB. This proves only technical execution.

2

Geometry passes basic checks

The binder is compact, contacts the requested region and has no severe clashes.

3

Sequence predicts back to the design

A structure predictor recovers a similar binder fold and interface with low uncertainty.

4

Orthogonal computational support

Multiple seeds or predictors agree, and explicit interface calculations show no obvious physical defects.

5

Experimental binding

Purified protein binds the target in a concentration-dependent assay. This is the first direct evidence of binding.

Recommended first-pass measurements
MeasurementQuestion answeredWhat it cannot prove
Hotspot contactsDid the backbone reach the requested surface?That those contacts are energetically favorable
Binder RMSDDoes prediction recover the designed binder fold?Experimental folding or binding
Interface PAEIs the relative chain placement confident?A calibrated dissociation constant
Buried surface areaIs there a substantial physical interface?Correct solvation thermodynamics
Clashes / polar satisfactionAre there obvious atomic defects?That slower rearrangements will not occur
BLI or SPR titrationDoes purified binder associate, and with what apparent Kd?Cellular function or universal specificity

Do not call the RFdiffusion trajectory dynamics

Its timesteps are generative noise levels, not femtoseconds or a thermodynamic path.

Do not interpret Boltz-2 affinity here

Boltz-2 can be explored as a protein-complex structure cross-check, but its small-molecule affinity output is not a protein–protein Kd.

Do not select after seeing only one seed

Generation and prediction are stochastic. Record seeds, settings, failures and all prespecified filters.

10 · Compact glossary

Terms worth keeping separate

Backbone

The repeating N–Cα–C atoms defining the main path of a protein chain. Side-chain identities are not determined by a backbone alone.

Residue frame

A local coordinate system describing a residue's position and orientation. RFdiffusion perturbs and denoises translations and rotations.

SE(3) equivariance

If an input complex is globally rotated or translated, the model's geometric output transforms consistently rather than changing its physical prediction.

Hotspot

In RFdiffusion, a labeled target residue used to direct the generated interface. It need not be a residue with experimentally measured energetic importance.

Contig

A compact specification of which residues are fixed, generated, connected or separated into different chains.

Inverse folding

Predicting amino-acid sequences compatible with a supplied three-dimensional protein backbone—the task performed by ProteinMPNN.

pAE / PAE

Predicted aligned error: uncertainty in relative residue positions after alignment. Interface PAE focuses on uncertainty between chains.

Kd

The equilibrium dissociation constant. A smaller Kd generally indicates tighter binding under the stated experimental conditions.
