Backbone generation ≠ sequence optimization

Our binding model belongs closer to ProteinMPNN

If the model reasons about hydrophobic groups, hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, charges, and buried polar atoms, its natural variables are amino-acid identity and side-chain geometry. Standard RFdiffusion mainly chooses the backbone; ProteinMPNN chooses which amino acids occupy it.

The intuition is correct—with one qualification

The chemistry model belongs primarily in sequence and side-chain design, but it should remain coupled to backbone quality. No amino-acid choice can rescue a backbone whose interface geometry cannot support the required contacts.

02 · What ProteinMPNN computes

A conditional distribution over sequences

ProteinMPNN is an inverse-folding graph neural network. Residues are nodes, spatially neighboring residues are connected by edges, and geometric messages are exchanged before amino acids are sampled.

  1. Encode geometry.N, Cα, C and O backbone coordinates define local residue environments and pairwise geometric features.
  2. Pass messages.The encoder integrates information from spatial neighbors in the binder and fixed target.
  3. Choose an order.A sampled decoding order determines which already selected identities are visible at each step.
  4. Predict logits.At position i, the decoder outputs one logit for each of the 20 standard amino acids.
  5. Sample a sequence.Repeating the procedure gives multiple sequences for the same backbone.

ProteinMPNN factorizes a backbone-conditioned sequence probability:

PMPNN(S | X, T) = ∏i P(si | X, T, Sprevious)
X
binder backbone
T
fixed target structure and sequence
S
designed binder sequence

03 · Ways to incorporate our model

Start outside ProteinMPNN; move inward only after validation

A generator can exploit a flawed score. Increasing integration increases efficiency, but also increases the danger of reward hacking and loss of sequence diversity.

Level 1Recommended first

Generate and rerank

MPNN samples→pack side chains→score→retain

Generate many ProteinMPNN sequences without changing the model. Build all-atom interfaces, evaluate them with our score, and rank them using a prespecified rule.

  • Score need not be differentiable.
  • Easy to compare against ordinary ProteinMPNN.
  • Separates score validation from generator modification.
Level 2Direct decoding bias

Modify amino-acid logits

At residue i, combine ProteinMPNN's structural preference with the predicted chemical value of each candidate amino acid:

ℓiguided(a) = ℓiMPNN(a) + β ΔFi(a)

The adjusted logits are passed through a softmax and sampled. This makes favorable choices more probable without discarding ProteinMPNN's learned sequence prior.

Level 3Coupled optimization

Optimize a complete interface

sequence→pack→score→mutate↻

Complete-sequence search captures coupled effects that per-residue logit bias misses: rotamer conflicts, cooperative hydrogen-bond networks, compensating mutations, and packing rearrangements.

Possible optimizers: Monte Carlo mutation, beam search, genetic search, or optimization over soft amino-acid probabilities.

Combined objective

Keep the protein prior; add the chemical objective

J(S) = log PMPNN(S | X,T) + β Finterface(S,X,T)

Equivalently, the guided sampler targets Pguided ∝ PMPNN exp(βFinterface). The coefficient β controls how strongly the new score can override ProteinMPNN.

04 · What should the score contain?

“Hydrophobic + hydrophilic” is a good start, but not yet a physical objective

Hydrophobic burial is not simply pairwise attraction, and polar contact is only favorable when donor/acceptor identity, distance, angle, solvation, and protonation are compatible.

↯

Penalize unfavorable rotamers and deformation of either partner.

This is a design score, not a unique or exact physical equation. Its terms and weights must be defined before evaluation and calibrated on data that were not used to construct it.

05 · Thermodynamic boundary

An interface score is not automatically ΔGbind

ΔGbind = Gcomplex − Gtarget − Gbinder
Solvent

Water displacement, dielectric response, salt, and protonation can change whether a contact is favorable.

Ensembles

Binding free energy averages over bound and unbound conformational states, not one minimized pose.

Entropy

Loss of translational, rotational, side-chain, and backbone freedom contributes to binding.

Specificity

A sticky interface can score well against one target pose yet bind many unintended surfaces.

Until calibrated against a defined experimental endpoint, call the output an interface-quality score or binding proxy—not a predicted equilibrium constant or binding free energy.
